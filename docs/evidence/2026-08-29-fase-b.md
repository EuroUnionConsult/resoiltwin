## What this note confirms, and what it does not

**It confirms:** that the connector authenticates against Copernicus with OAuth client
credentials, that it asks the Statistical API for per-polygon statistics with the
geometry reprojected to UTM 29N, that it writes the result as `satellite_observed`
observations with full provenance, that the same `/timeseries` route returns satellite
series or field readings depending on the metric asked for, that re-running the same
request writes not one new row, and that each run leaves a record of its own in the
`ingestion_jobs` table.

**What it does not confirm, and this has to be said before any number:**

1. **The satellite does not measure soil moisture.** NDVI, NDMI and NDRE are indices
   computed over surface reflectance — they respond to vegetation cover, not to the
   water content of the soil. The soil moisture that exists in this system comes from
   the probes used on the ground at Turcifal, is `observed_screening`, and remains the
   project's only soil measurement. The spectral indices are **landscape context**:
   they say what state the vegetation around is in, not what is happening beneath it.
2. **None of this is agronomic validation.** There is no calibration, no established
   correlation between index and soil variable, no series long enough to claim one. It
   is 28 days of August 2026 over two areas.
3. **There is no pixel-level cloud mask.** See the section "How cloud is (and is not)
   handled" — it is the most important limitation of this ingestion.

`rows_written = 0` in both, and the count in the database does not move: 85 before, 85
after. The two numbers matter for different reasons — the first is what the service
claims, the second is what the database holds. An implementation that counted wrong
would give two different answers.

The `request_hash` is identical to the first run in both cases. That is what identifies
"the same request": same area of interest, same window, same collection, same
processing version, same resolution, same cloud threshold. Change any one of those six
and you get another hash — and another request.

**The hash is not the deduplication key, and the difference matters.** What decides
whether a row is written is the constraint `uq_observation_identity`, over
`(site, plot, observed_at, metric, source_type, processing_version)`. The
`request_hash`, the `max_cloud` and the `resolution_m` **do not enter it**. Concrete
consequence: a sync with `max_cloud: 50` over the same window is another request, has
another hash, costs another call to Copernicus — and writes **exactly zero rows**. The
rows already stored stand, and the `evidence` is not rewritten with the new threshold.
Reprocessing a window with different parameters requires changing the
`processing_version` or deleting the old rows; repeating the sync is not enough.

## What was collected, exactly

| | |
|---|---|
| Source | Copernicus Data Space Ecosystem, Statistical API (`sh.dataspace.copernicus.eu/api/v1/statistics`) |
| Authentication | OAuth 2.0 *client credentials*, token reused until 60 s before expiry |
| Collection | `sentinel-2-l2a` (Sentinel-2 MSI, level L2A, surface reflectance) |
| Indices | NDVI = (B08−B04)/(B08+B04); NDMI = (B08−B11)/(B08+B11); NDRE = (B8A−B05)/(B8A+B05) |
| Requested resolution | 10 m (`resx`/`resy`) |
| Processing CRS | EPSG:32629 (UTM 29N) — the database stores EPSG:4326 and the reprojection is done at ingestion |
| Aggregation | `P1D` — one value per acquisition day, spatial mean over the polygon |
| Cloud filter | `maxCloudCoverage: 30` at the **scene** level |
| Pixel-level mask | `dataMask` only (absence of data). **Not a cloud mask** |
| Processing version | `s2-ndvi-ndmi-ndre-v1+f03f9beed32d` (evalscript version + truncated SHA-256 of the script that actually ran) |

**On the 10 m resolution:** only B04 and B08 are natively 10 m. B05, B8A and B11 are
20 m in the Sentinel-2 product. Asking for them at 10 m makes the server resample —
**it does not create detail**. NDVI is the only one of the three indices computed
entirely from natively 10 m bands; NDMI and NDRE each carry one resampled band.

**On the `source_type`:** the indices enter as `satellite_observed` and not as
`derived`. An index computed over the reflectance of an acquisition is still a
measurement by the satellite, not a product computed over other observations in the
database. `derived` is reserved for the latter — in the current system, the 4 VPD
values.

### Pixel counts per area of interest

| Area of interest | `valid_pixels` as stored | `no_data_pixels` as stored | **Pixels that contributed** |
|---|---|---|---|
| `EUC-TUR-EO1` | 62 750 | 0 | **62 750** |
| `EUC-PTO-EO1` | 2 501 | 1 406 | **1 095** |

The third column is the one that matters, and it is **not** the one stored in the
database. The `valid_pixels` key of the `evidence` does not hold the number of valid
pixels, despite the name.

**Mind the field name.** What the `valid_pixels` key of the `evidence` holds is the
`sampleCount` returned by the Statistical API, which is the number of pixels in the
grid covering the area of interest — **including** those discarded by `dataMask`. The
pixels that actually contributed to each average are `valid_pixels − no_data_pixels`:
62 750 at Turcifal and **1 095** at Porto, not 2 501. At Turcifal the two numbers
coincide because the area is a rectangle practically aligned with the processing grid,
and nothing is discarded; that is why the difference only shows at Porto, whose polygon
is irregular and occupies about 40% of its bounding box. Porto's 1 095 pixels are
consistent with the 1 014 pixels the polygon area predicts (101 438 m² / 100 m² per
pixel), the difference explained by boundary pixels. **The key is badly named and will
be corrected in the next phase**; it is recorded here so that nobody quotes 2 501 as
Porto's valid pixel count.

## How cloud is (and is not) handled

This is the most important limitation of this ingestion, and it needs to be explicit:

- **At the scene level**, the request rejects acquisitions with more than 30% cloud
  cover (`maxCloudCoverage: 30`).
- **At the pixel level, there is no cloud mask.** The evalscript uses only the
  `dataMask`, which marks absence of data, not cloud or cloud shadow. A scene with 25%
  cloud enters whole, and its cloudy pixels enter the average.

This has a concrete and visible consequence in this series. Querying the Catalog for
the same area and window (14 scenes across 11 dates, all with cloud ≤ 30%):

> **Provenance of this block.** Unlike every other number in this note, the table below
> **does not re-derive from the database**: it came from a Catalog query made on
> 28/08/2026 during this verification, and the response is not persisted anywhere.
> Reproducing it means repeating the query. Storing `eo:cloud_cover` per date in each
> observation's `evidence` would close this gap and is recorded as technical debt for
> the next phase.

### The 2026-08-24 anomaly, and what cannot be concluded from it

> ⚠️ **This section is superseded** by the note
> [`2026-08-29-mascara-scl.md`](2026-08-29-mascara-scl.md), which applied the
> pixel-level mask this section says is missing. In short: 91.53% of the area was
> cloud, shadow or cirrus, and the **drop** in NDVI and NDRE is **confirmed** as
> contamination. What the newer note does *not* conclude is the level of the index
> under the cloud — the 8.47% of pixels that remain are not a sample of the area. The
> text below is left as it stands, as a record of what could be said on 28/08.

**2026-08-24 is the anomalous date of the series.** NDVI falls from ~0.46 to 0.2111,
NDRE falls by half (0.3256 → 0.1531) and NDMI rises 6× (0.0303 → 0.1847). It is also
the cloudiest scene of the window — 29.94%, just under the 30% threshold that would
have excluded it — and the day of the field campaign, the one on which the screening
probe read the highest moisture of the three days (8/10 on the grass) and relative
humidity reached 80%.

There are therefore two possible readings, and one has to be exact about what the data
supports:

1. **Real signal.** The satellite saw water in the landscape on the same day the probe
   saw it in the soil. NDMI rising with moisture rising is the physically expected
   direction.
2. **Cloud and shadow contamination.** With no pixel-level mask, the cloudy pixels of an
   accepted scene enter the average, and NDVI down + NDRE down + NDMI up is the pattern
   that produces.

**Neither has been excluded, and the scene cloud percentage is no help in deciding
between them.** The table above carries the counter-example itself: **2026-08-01 has a
scene at 29.13% cloud — practically the same as the 29.94% of 24/08 — and entirely
normal values, with the highest NDVI of the whole series (0.4851), NDMI 0.0543 and NDRE
0.3395.** The explanation is simple: `eo:cloud_cover` is a property of the whole scene,
which covers a far larger area than ours; the cloud may be entirely outside Campo Real.
A high scene-level percentage does not imply contamination of the area, and a low one
does not rule it out.

What is recorded, and it is all the data supports:

- the values for 2026-08-24 are **anomalous** relative to the rest of the series;
- **neither** of the two explanations — real signal or contamination — has been
  excluded;
- excluding one of them requires a **pixel-level cloud mask** (the SCL band, or
  equivalent), which this pipeline does not apply.

This is deliberately weaker than either of the two claims that could be made from this
series, and it is the only one that holds. In particular, **this note does not claim
that there was a soil↔satellite correspondence on 24/08** — nor that there was not. Any
record of the project that claims it is going beyond what this data shows, and must be
corrected.

## Known limits, beyond the three already declared

- **No Sentinel-1.** Optical only. Days with cloud above the threshold have no coverage
  at all — which is exactly what radar would solve, and it is left for a later phase.
- **No scheduling.** The sync is triggered by hand. Nothing runs it on its own until the
  Phase E infrastructure exists.
- **No authentication on the routes.** The API must not be exposed publicly at this
  stage. Anyone with access to the route can fire synchronisations, which spends
  Copernicus quota.
- **No weather layer.** Precipitation and reference evapotranspiration do not exist in
  the system. Without them there is no water balance, and without a water balance no
  spectral index converts into a statement about water in the soil.
- **The window is 28 days.** An August series has no seasonality, no wet/dry contrast,
  and supports no claim about a trend.
